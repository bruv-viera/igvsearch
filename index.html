How well does this map answer the question?

You will be shown a series of search queries. Under each one, a set of interactive geovisualisations found for that query. Your job is to judge how well each map matches the query it sits under.

Run by Viera · For any questions, or to withdraw: viera.mlilo@tum.de
Please use a computer. A laptop or desktop with a reasonably large screen makes the map previews far easier to judge. The study works on a phone, but small previews make the task harder and slower.

About the study

My name is Viera. I am an MSc Cartography student at the Technical University of Munich (TUM), carrying out my thesis research at the Technische Universität Dresden (TUD).

For my thesis — Evaluating a search algorithm for interactive geovisualisations — I need people to judge how well the maps the algorithm finds match the search queries. That is what your ratings provide. There are no right or wrong answers; your own honest judgement is exactly what I am looking for.

What you will do

  1. Read the search query shown at the top of the screen.
  2. Look at the map preview, its title and description. You can open the real map in a new tab if you want a closer look.
  3. Rate how well that map matches the query, from 0 to 5.
  4. For a few maps, add one sentence on why you gave that rating.

You will rate 12 map–query pairs in total, and write a one-sentence comment on 3 of them. It takes roughly 5–10 minutes. You can go back and change any answer before you finish, and you can close the page and return later — your answers stay on this device until sent by email.

Consent and your data

  • Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time by simply closing the page.
  • No personal data is collected. You are identified only by a short random code such as p-a1b, generated on your device.
  • Your answers stay in this browser until you choose to send them at the end.
  • At the end you will send your answers to me, Viera, at viera.mlilo@tum.de — either with one click, or by email. They will be used only for this thesis, reported in aggregate, and not shared with third parties.
  • Questions or withdrawal: viera.mlilo@tum.de
